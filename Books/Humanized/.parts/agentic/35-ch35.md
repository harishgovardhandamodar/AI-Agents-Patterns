## Episodic Memory Pattern Overview

Store whole past episodes (task + outcome) and replay them for case-based decisions.

**Episodic Memory** is catalogued in the **Memory & Knowledge** part of the catalog (id `episodic-memory`). It is tagged for support, general and is assembled from 2 components.

Record the episode with its outcome label; retrieve similar episodes; adapt what worked, avoid what failed. The label is the load-bearing part, not the similarity score: a lucky bad decision stored as success gets faithfully replayed as wisdom, and similarity retrieval keeps recommending the same flawed strategy. Ground truth labels plus human audit of high-impact episodes are what separate a case library from a superstition engine.

**Definition 1 (Grounded episode).** An episode $e = (\mathrm{task}, \mathrm{outcome}, \mathrm{label})$ is *grounded* iff (i) its outcome label derives from verified ground truth (external results, not the agent's self-report), (ii) high-impact episodes pass human audit, and (iii) failed strategies are marked as failures to avoid — not merely absent from the wins.

**Theorem 1 (Mislabeled replay compounds).** Under similarity retrieval, a mislabeled episode (lucky bad decision stored as success) is retrieved for every similar future task — the error replicates at retrieval rate until relabeled, because similarity matches tasks, not truth.

*Proof.* Retrieval keys on task similarity: similar tasks fetch the episode regardless of its label's accuracy. Each replay applies the flawed strategy, and if its outcome is recorded the same way, the index gains corroborating (false) evidence — availability bias as a data structure. Ground-truth labeling breaks the cycle at write time (the lucky win is labeled luck, not skill); human audit of high-impact episodes catches what automation missed; marking failures explicitly turns the index into avoidance guidance rather than repetition fuel. Verify labels against external results; sample episodes linked to analogical-reasoning and long-term memory; confirm failed strategies are marked so replay guides avoidance rather than repetition. ∎
