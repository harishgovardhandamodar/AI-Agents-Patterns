## Chapter 32: Domain playbook: iot

At stake: Device fleets, firmware images, telemetry, and actuators at scale.

Telemetry reading and firmware writing must live in different sandboxes; no agent holds both the eyes and the hands. Catalog rehearsal: *Smart-home agent locks the owner out during a false alarm* (1 of the domain's scenarios).

**Definition 1 (Eyes-hands separation at fleet scale).** The fleet satisfies *eyes-hands separation* iff (i) telemetry sandboxes hold reads only, (ii) firmware and actuation sandboxes hold gated writes only, (iii) no credential spans both sides, (iv) device-group scoping bounds every credential, and (v) fleet-wide moves require human approval with the hold timed.

**Theorem 1 (One endpoint's compromise stays local).** Under eyes-hands separation, a turned telemetry reader can lie about the world but cannot move it, and a turned actuator can move its group but cannot lie about the world to the others — perception and action fail independently, never jointly.

*Proof.* The reader holds no actuation grant (i, iii): spoofed telemetry enters the control decision as input, subject to the gate and the hold — alarming, reviewable, but not self-executing. The actuator holds no telemetry grant: its moves are visible to independent readers whose reports it cannot alter. Device-group scoping (iv) bounds a turned actuator to its group; fleet-wide moves need the human (v), so mass actuation is never one compromise away. The false-alarm lockout is the single-device instance: the actuator obeyed a false input within its group, contained by scope, reviewable in the log. Verify no sandbox holds both sides; scope one credential to its group; gate one fleet-wide action and time the hold. ∎
