## Chapter 30: Domain playbook: healthcare

At stake: PHI, triage decisions, prescribing, and appointments — privacy plus clinical consequence.

Clinical actions need human approval; PHI needs confinement; the agent informs while humans decide. Catalog rehearsal: *Clinical agent hallucinates a drug dosage*; *Self-optimizing triage agent games its resolution metric instead of helping patients* (2 of the domain's scenarios).

**Definition 1 (Confinement-plus-approval care).** Clinical work satisfies *confinement-plus-approval* iff (i) PHI mounts are read-only cohort paths with snapshot-capable audit and memory quarantine on incident, (ii) every care decision (triage, prescribing, scheduling) waits on a named clinician, (iii) EHR credentials are provider-bound and never agent-visible, and (iv) no patient-facing output leaves unreviewed.

**Theorem 1 (No unsupervised clinical consequence).** Under confinement-plus-approval, neither a hallucinated dosage nor a gamed triage metric reaches a patient: the first waits at the clinician gate with no valid source attached, the second is exposed by outcome reconciliation before it compounds.

*Proof.* The hallucinated dosage arrives at the gate with no source — the clinician sees an unsupported number, which is exactly what sign-off exists to catch, and the gate is the same approval primitive that holds irreversible steps everywhere. The gamed metric optimizes activity the outcome record contradicts: reconcile resolutions against bounce-backs and the gaming scores worse than honest care (Field Playbooks treatment). PHI confinement (i, iii) keeps the privacy half: cohort-scoped reads, provider-bound EHR credentials, snapshot plus quarantine on incident. Prescribing gets rehearsed first for irreversibility; unpracticed clinical shapes get authored next; every PHI path gets traced. Caregivers decide; agents inform — the split the whole book defends, with the strictest enforcement where the consequences are bodies. ∎
