## Chapter 46: Locked at creation vs hot-reload

Filesystem, landlock strictness and process identity lock at creation or startup because changing the world mid-run would invalidate every assumption the launch confirmation established. Network rules and middlewares reload live because conversations legitimately evolve. Sort every line of a policy by timing and defend each placement — mutability scheduled in advance is still attack surface.

Rehearse both transitions: lock a filesystem rule and confirm a mid-run change refuses; update a network rule and confirm hot application. Trust the timing table only after testing it, and re-test on every runtime you deploy.

**Definition 1 (Timing-sorted policy).** A policy is *timing-sorted* iff every line is classified locked (filesystem, landlock strictness, process identity — fixed at creation/startup) or live (network rules, middlewares — hot-reloadable), each placement is defended, and both behaviors are rehearsed (locked refuses mid-run change, live applies it).

**Theorem 1 (Immutability is the guarantee).** Locked settings are trustworthy exactly because they cannot change: the supervisor's launch confirmation, the freeze's protection, and the audit's baseline all assume the world they verified — an assumption hot-reload would silently break.

*Proof.* Launch confirmation verifies the boundary as built; if filesystem or identity could change mid-run, the confirmation would attest to a world that no longer exists. The freeze protects the confirmed boundary; mutable foundations make the freeze a snapshot of drift. Audit baselines compare current state against launch state; mutable anchors make every comparison meaningless. Live-tunable network rules are safe precisely because the anchors do not move — conversations evolve against fixed ground. Mutability scheduled in advance is still attack surface: it is just attack surface with a timetable, so defend each live placement as deliberately as each locked one. Rehearse a mid-run lock refusal and a hot application; test the timing table on every runtime; list what cannot change mid-run and confirm the list is long. ∎
