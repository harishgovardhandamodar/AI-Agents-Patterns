## Chapter 29: Domain playbook: governance

At stake: Policy documents, audit trails, and access reviews — the controls that judge the other controls.

The agent that reviews access must never be able to edit the grants it reviews. Catalog rehearsal: *Voting council gamed by duplicate personas; quorum captured* (1 of the domain's scenarios).

**Definition 1 (Reviewer-noninterference).** Governance satisfies *reviewer noninterference* iff (i) review sandboxes mount policies and trails read-only (write: [] — nothing to edit, even legitimately), (ii) no network egress exists for the reviewer (network_policies: [] — findings cannot leak or self-publish), (iii) grants reviewed by the agent are never grantable by it, and (iv) human-majority decides every grant with agents drafting and flagging only.

**Theorem 1 (Oversight the overseen cannot edit).** Under reviewer noninterference, a turned governance agent can misread, miss, or misrank — but cannot alter the grants, trails, or policies it reviews, nor exfiltrate its findings: the worst case is a wrong opinion, never a changed control.

*Proof.* Editing requires write access the sandbox does not mount (i) — the policy documents and audit trails are read-only to every governance agent by construction. Exfiltration requires egress the sandbox does not have (ii) — findings stay inside until a human carries them out. Self-granting requires grant authority the reviewer never holds (iii). What remains — misreading, missing, misranking — is bounded by human-majority decision (iv): agents draft and flag, humans decide, so a wrong agent opinion faces a human verdict before it matters. Suppressed findings (the counsel's rehearsal priority) meet the append-only trail: the review that happened is recorded regardless of what the reviewer claims. Verify append-only writes are unmodifiable; rehearse the suppressed finding; confirm segregation between reviewed and reviewer. ∎
