**Definition 1 (Matter-disjoint practice).** The legal deployment satisfies *matter-disjointness* iff (i) each matter lives in its own sandbox with no shared context, (ii) writes are confined to that matter's drafts, (iii) no network egress exists (network_policies: [] — research happens through governed channels or not at all), and (iv) every external communication leaves only after licensed-human review.

**Theorem 1 (Privilege cannot cross matters).** Under matter-disjointness, privileged material from matter $A$ cannot appear in matter $B$'s workspace, drafts, discovery, or filings — regardless of agent intent, prompt injection, or retrieval error.

*Proof.* Cross-matter movement requires either shared context (refused: disjoint sandboxes), network transfer (refused: no egress), or the human review path (licensed review that checks privilege designations). Retrieval can only return what the sandbox can read — matter $A$'s paths are unmounted in $B$'s sandbox. With twenty patterns of legal tooling against one rehearsed story, the discipline matters more than the coverage: confirm no sandbox spans two matters, gate every filing, and write the matter boundaries measurably so the goal comparator can judge crossings. A leaked privileged document cannot be unleaked; a missed filing deadline cannot be unmissed. ∎
