**Definition 1 (Valve-separated sandboxes).** The deployment satisfies *valve separation* iff (i) no sandbox holds both telemetry read and control write, (ii) every control action requires human approval, (iii) historian appends are write-once with snapshot before quarantine, and (iv) gate holds on control actions are timed against process time constants.

**Theorem 1 (No single sandbox actuates the plant).** Under valve separation, no turned agent — however complete its telemetry picture — can move a physical control without crossing into a second sandbox, a human approval, and a timed gate.

*Proof.* The telemetry sandbox holds reads only (write: [] — nothing to actuate with, and no control grant to borrow). Actuation lives in the control sandbox behind human approval (ii) and the timed hold (iv): the gate's latency is measured against the process time constant it guards, so a too-slow gate is caught by the metric rather than trusted by hope. The historian (iii) preserves the true record through drift episodes — manipulated telemetry history cannot rewrite what the append-only log recorded. Verify no sandbox holds both sides of the valve; time one gate hold; confirm the historian is append-only. Physics-adjacent control gets the strongest runtime because consequences persist after the incident ends. ∎
