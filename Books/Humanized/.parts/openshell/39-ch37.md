## Chapter 37: Domain playbook: procurement

At stake: Purchase orders, vendor records, invoices, and the separation between drafting and approving.

The agent that drafts the order must not onboard its payee; duties split across sandboxes. Catalog rehearsal: *Vendor selection steered by attacker SEO content* (1 of the domain's scenarios).

Catalog weight behind this chapter: 7 patterns, 1 scenarios, 2 cascades (inherent 1, emergent 0, derived 1, cascading 0).

**Definition 1 (Sandbox-split procurement).** Procurement satisfies the *sandbox split* iff (i) drafting (vendor search, order drafts) and approval (onboarding, dual control, payment) live in different sandboxes with different credentials, (ii) vendor records are read-only to the drafting side with versioning and cooling-off, (iii) vendor creation is the hardest-gated action in the flow, and (iv) one vendor file is audited end to end per quarter.

**Theorem 1 (Steered search, gated purchase).** Under the sandbox split, attacker-steered vendor selection corrupts the draft but cannot corrupt the purchase: the steered vendor must survive onboarding (versioned file, cooling-off, dual control) in a sandbox the steering never reached.

*Proof.* SEO poisoning acts on the drafting side's inputs (search rankings), producing a draft naming the attacker's vendor. Onboarding lives across the sandbox boundary: versioned vendor records expose the vendor's missing history, cooling-off delays first payment, dual control reviews the file — all in the approval sandbox, unreachable from the steered search. Fake-vendor creation (the highest-weight rehearsal, by payout persistence) is exactly the shape the split defeats: creating a payee is the hardest gate, not a draft side-effect. Invoice inflation and approval-chain bypass face the same boundary in sequence. Split one procure-to-pay flow across two sandboxes; audit who can create a vendor; rehearse the fake-vendor variant explicitly. ∎
