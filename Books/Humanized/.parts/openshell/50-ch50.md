## Chapter 50: What OpenShell does not solve

The central limit, stated plainly: OpenShell governs what the agent can touch, not what the agent wants. Drift pursued entirely within policy is invisible to every fence; injection and poisoned context arrive inside allowed traffic; collusion over allowed relays needs pair-level judgment no fence provides. Containment bounds the damage while other instruments — goal comparison, prompt defenses, pair review — do the judging.

And the operator gap: revocation, handoff, remediation and release need staffed humans on real clocks, and no runtime primitive supplies staffing. The last mile is people, drills and evidence — budget for it or inherit it as incident debt.

**Definition 1 (Touch-vs-want boundary).** OpenShell's jurisdiction is *touch*: files, syscalls, connections, credentials, generations — everything the agent can reach. *Want* — objectives, beliefs, coordinated intent, prompt-injected goals — belongs to detectors, comparators, reviewers, and humans. The boundary between them is the honest edge of this book.

**Theorem 1 (Contained but unjudged).** Under touch-only containment, the worst case is bounded damage with unjudged intent: the fence guarantees the blast radius, but only the paired instruments (goal comparison for drift, prompt defenses for injection, pair review for collusion) determine what the bounded activity meant — and the operator gap (staffed humans on real clocks) determines whether the judgment happens in time.

*Proof.* Touch controls constrain action spaces, never objective spaces (Part II residual, six times demonstrated): a decayed objective pursued entirely within policy triggers no fence, by construction. Injection arrives inside allowed traffic — the fence sees permitted bytes, not poisoned intent. Allowed-relay collusion is policy-compliant coordination — the fence sees the relay it approved. In each case containment bounds what happens next (blast radius fixed) while judgment requires the paired instrument (comparator, prompt defense, pair review). The operator gap is the final term: even perfect instruments need staffed humans to answer their findings on the clock — revocation, handoff, remediation, release. No runtime primitive supplies staffing, so the incident workflow's human steps are load-bearing by design. Pair every deployment with the detector suite; name the human owner for every deadline; budget the last mile or inherit it as debt. ∎
