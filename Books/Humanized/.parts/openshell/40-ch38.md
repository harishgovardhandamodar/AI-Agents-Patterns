**Definition 1 (Reconstructible public decisions).** Public-sector work satisfies *reconstructibility* iff (i) case records mount read-only with versioned inputs, (ii) every benefit, permit, and enforcement output carries a named official's recorded decision, (iii) decisions and trails are append-only with snapshot-capable audit, and (iv) rehearsal covers eligibility distortion and records mishandling as distinct shapes with results published where oversight reads them.

**Theorem 1 (Unroutable but auditable).** Under reconstructibility, citizens who cannot route around an error retain the three substitutes for exit: published criteria to check against, versioned records to appeal with, and named officials answerable for the outcome.

*Proof.* Read-only versioned inputs (i) fix what the decision was based on — the citizen and the auditor see the same record. Named officials (ii) fix who decided — accountability attaches to a person, not a pipeline. Append-only audit (iii) fixes what happened — the trail cannot be rewritten after the fact. Eligibility distortion rehearsal (iv) tests the decision path; records-mishandling rehearsal tests the archive path. Together they convert unroutable-around power into auditable power: the citizen cannot choose another provider, but can check the criterion, appeal with the versioned record, and name the official.
